Nicholas Ray

USA · The Golden Age

Classical Hollywood

Born in Galesville, Wisconsin, in 1911 and died in 1979, Nicholas Ray studied under Frank Lloyd Wright, worked in the theatre and radio, and was an assistant to Elia Kazan. His films concern lonely rebels, wounded men and misfits, filmed in expressive colour and widescreen. Rebel Without a Cause (1955), with James Dean, defined the teenage alienation of the 1950s, and he was a hero to the French critics at Cahiers du cinéma, who admired his emotion and his visual flair. Godard said that cinema is Nicholas Ray.
