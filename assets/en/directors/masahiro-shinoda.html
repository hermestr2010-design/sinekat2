Masahiro Shinoda

Japan · The Modern Masters

Japanese New Wave

Born in Gifu in 1931, Masahiro Shinoda worked as an assistant to Yasujiro Ozu at Shochiku and became a leading figure of the Japanese New Wave. His films often turn on a loner, a stylised setting and the music of Tōru Takemitsu. Pale Flower (1964) is a noir about a yakuza who falls for a gambler's daughter. Double Suicide (1969) retells a puppet-theatre tragedy with black-clad stagehands moving through the scenes. He also made the historical drama Assassination. In 2003 he made his last film, Spy Sorge, and retired.
