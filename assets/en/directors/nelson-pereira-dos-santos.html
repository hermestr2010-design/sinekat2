Nelson Pereira dos Santos

Brazil · The Modern Masters

Latin American Cinema

Born in São Paulo in 1928 and died in Rio de Janeiro in 2018, Nelson Pereira dos Santos was a founder of Brazil's Cinema Novo, and made Rio, 40 Degrees (1955), shot on location with non-professional actors and influenced by Italian neorealism. His adaptation of Graciliano Ramos's novel, Vidas Secas (1963), about a family of drought refugees, is a cornerstone of the movement. He went on to make How Tasty Was My Little Frenchman (1971), a cannibal satire on colonialism, and Memories of Prison (1984). In 2006 he became the first film-maker elected to the Brazilian Academy of Letters.
