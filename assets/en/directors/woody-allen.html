Woody Allen

USA · The Modern Masters

American Independent Cinema

Born in Brooklyn in 1935, Allan Stewart Konigsberg became Woody Allen as a teenage joke writer, then a stand-up comedian, playwright and film-maker. His early comedies, such as Take the Money and Run and Sleeper, were slapstick, but Annie Hall (1977), which won the Oscar for best picture and best director, turned to autobiographical romance and a more literary style. Manhattan (1979), Hannah and Her Sisters (1986) and Crimes and Misdemeanors (1989) are some of his most admired works. He has been nominated for more writing Oscars than anyone else, and has been the subject of allegations by Dylan Farrow, which he denies and which led to no charges.
