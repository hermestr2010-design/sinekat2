Claude Lelouch

France · The Modern Masters

French New Wave

Born in Paris in 1937, Claude Lelouch started shooting films as a teenager and spent his money on a short film that was a failure. After several flops, he made A Man and a Woman (1966), a low-budget love story between a widower racing driver and a widowed script girl, with a lush score by Francis Lai, and it won the Palme d'Or and two Oscars. He is prolific, often shoots with his own camera and does a lot of improvisation, and his later films include Les Uns et les Autres (1981) and Les Misérables (1995). A Man and a Woman: Twenty Years Later followed in 1986.
