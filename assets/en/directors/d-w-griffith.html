D.W. Griffith

USA · The Silent Era

Early and Pioneering Cinema

Born in Kentucky in 1875 and died in 1948, Griffith did more than anyone to turn early American film into a storytelling art, developing cross-cutting, the close-up and the long feature. His The Birth of a Nation (1915) was a technical landmark but is also notorious for its racist portrayal of Black Americans and its glorification of the Ku Klux Klan, a legacy that remains central to any honest account of his career.
