Abel Gance

France · The Silent Era

Early and Pioneering Cinema

Born in Paris in 1889 and died in 1981, Gance was an experimenter who kept inventing new film techniques, from rapid cutting and a hand-held camera to the three-screen Polyvision of his epic Napoléon. His anti-war film J'accuse used real soldiers on leave from the front. Napoléon was heavily cut after its release and was restored in stages over many decades.

Filmography

  • Mater Dolorosa (1917)
  • J'accuse (1919)
  • La Roue (1923)
  • Napoléon (1927)
  • Lucrezia Borgia (1935)
  • Un grand amour de Beethoven (1936)
  • Austerlitz (1960)
