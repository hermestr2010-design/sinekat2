Volker Schlöndorff

Germany · The Modern Masters

New German Cinema

Born in Wiesbaden in 1939, Volker Schlöndorff studied in France and worked as assistant to Louis Malle, Alain Resnais and Jean-Pierre Melville before returning to Germany. A key figure of New German Cinema, he specialised in adapting literary works, often with political edge: Young Törless (1966) from Musil, The Lost Honour of Katharina Blum (1975) with Margarethe von Trotta from Böll, and The Tin Drum (1979) from Günter Grass, which shared the Palme d'Or and won the Oscar for best foreign language film.
