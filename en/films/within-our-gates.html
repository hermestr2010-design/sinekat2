Plot

Sylvia Landry, a young Black schoolteacher, travels north to Boston to raise funds for a school for Black children in the South. Her struggle is intercut with a flashback to her own past, which includes a lynching and an attempted assault. Oscar Micheaux's 1920 film was a direct reply to D.W. Griffith's The Birth of a Nation and is the earliest surviving feature film by an African American director. Censors in Chicago tried to stop its release because of its violence, and the film is an important record of Black independent cinema.
