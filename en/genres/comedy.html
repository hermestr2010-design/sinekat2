Comedy

Comedy is one of cinema's first genres and one of its most inventive. Silent clowns such as Chaplin, Keaton and Lloyd built whole stories from timing and physical risk; Lubitsch and Sturges brought wit and sophistication; screwball comedies of the 1930s paired fast talk with romance. Later strands include the absurd, the satirical and the comedy of embarrassment, found from Tati's France to Bollywood and modern Turkey.

Where
The United States, France, Italy, Britain, India, Japan, Turkey
When
From the 1890s to today
Key figures
Charlie Chaplin, Buster Keaton, Ernst Lubitsch, Preston Sturges, Billy Wilder, Jacques Tati, Woody Allen
Landmark films
The Gold Rush (1925), Duck Soup (1933), Some Like It Hot (1959), Playtime (1967)
Legacy
Taught film its sense of rhythm, timing and visual gag construction

What to look for

  • Physical gags planned shot by shot
  • Rapid, overlapping dialogue
  • Mistaken identities and social satire
  • Running jokes that pay off late
  • Warmth under the mockery
