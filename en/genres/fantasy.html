Fantasy

Fantasy brings myth, magic and dream logic to the screen. It stretches from Méliès's trick films to Cocteau's Beauty and the Beast, from the Technicolor wonder of The Wizard of Oz and The Thief of Bagdad to Miyazaki's animated worlds and the digital epics of the 2000s. The best examples treat the impossible with complete seriousness, so that the rules of the invented world feel real.

Where
France, the United States, Britain, Japan, Mexico
When
From the 1900s; major waves in the 1930s–40s and after 2000
Key figures
Georges Méliès, Jean Cocteau, Michael Powell, Hayao Miyazaki, Guillermo del Toro, Peter Jackson
Landmark films
The Thief of Bagdad (1924), Beauty and the Beast (1946), The Wizard of Oz (1939), Pan's Labyrinth (2006)
Legacy
Showed that cinema can build whole worlds, not just record the real one

What to look for

  • Myths and fairy tales retold
  • Worlds with their own rules and logic
  • Costume, creature and set design
  • Dream and reality overlapping
  • Journeys that change the hero
