Film-Noir

Film noir is a mood more than a plot: shadowy cities, fatalistic heroes, deadly women and corruption beneath the surface. It arose in 1940s Hollywood from hard-boiled fiction and from émigré directors such as Lang, Wilder and Siodmak, who brought German Expressionist lighting. Double Indemnity, The Big Sleep and Touch of Evil defined it; it later returned as neo-noir in Chinatown and Blade Runner.

Where
The United States; later France and Japan
When
Classic period 1941–1958; neo-noir from the 1970s
Key figures
Billy Wilder, Fritz Lang, Robert Siodmak, Orson Welles, Jules Dassin, Jean-Pierre Melville
Landmark films
Double Indemnity (1944), The Big Sleep (1946), Out of the Past (1947), Touch of Evil (1958)
Legacy
Its look and attitude are still used to signal moral darkness

What to look for

  • Low-key lighting and strong shadows
  • Voice-over and flashback
  • Femmes fatales and doomed protagonists
  • Night streets, rain and neon
  • Cynicism toward institutions
