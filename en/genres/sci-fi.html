Sci-Fi

Science fiction uses invented futures, machines and aliens to ask questions about the present. Méliès and Lang's Metropolis established its spectacle; the 1950s cycle channelled Cold War fear; Kubrick's 2001 and Tarkovsky's Solaris made it contemplative; Star Wars and Blade Runner reshaped it for the blockbuster era. Its effects work has driven technical change across all of cinema.

Where
The United States, Britain, the Soviet Union, Japan, France
When
From 1902; major waves in the 1950s, 1970s and after 2000
Key figures
Georges Méliès, Fritz Lang, Stanley Kubrick, Andrei Tarkovsky, Ridley Scott, Denis Villeneuve
Landmark films
A Trip to the Moon (1902), Metropolis (1927), 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968), Blade Runner (1982)
Legacy
Drove special-effects innovation and gave cinema its main vehicle for speculating about the future

What to look for

  • Technology as threat or promise
  • Strange worlds built with design and effects
  • Human questions inside big ideas
  • Dystopian cities and sealed environments
  • Time, memory and identity as puzzles
