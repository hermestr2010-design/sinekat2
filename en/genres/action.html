Action

Action films are built from movement: chases, fights and stunts staged for maximum clarity and thrill. The roots lie in silent serials and slapstick, in Douglas Fairbanks's swashbucklers and in Kurosawa's samurai pictures; later came Hong Kong's martial-arts and gunplay choreography, Hollywood's 1980s muscle heroes and the digitally enhanced blockbusters of today. Craft, not just scale, separates the great from the merely loud.

Where
The United States, Hong Kong, Japan, India, France
When
From the 1910s; peak popularity from the 1980s
Key figures
Douglas Fairbanks, Akira Kurosawa, Sam Peckinpah, Bruce Lee, John Woo, James Cameron, George Miller
Landmark films
Seven Samurai (1954), Enter the Dragon (1973), Die Hard (1988), Mad Max: Fury Road (2015)
Legacy
Defined the modern blockbuster and the art of staging physical spectacle

What to look for

  • Clear geography in chases and fights
  • Stunts performed for the camera
  • Editing rhythm tied to movement
  • Heroes defined by what they do
  • Set pieces that escalate
