Thriller

Thrillers run on anticipation: a threat the audience can feel before the characters do. Hitchcock defined the form with wrong-man chases, voyeurism and the famous distinction between surprise and suspense. Later filmmakers added political paranoia (Pakula, Costa-Gavras), psychological unease (Polanski, De Palma) and global versions from Korea, France and Scandinavia. Camera placement, editing rhythm and sound do most of the work.

Where
The United States, Britain, France, Italy, South Korea
When
From the 1920s; classical peak 1940s to 1960s
Key figures
Alfred Hitchcock, Fritz Lang, Henri-Georges Clouzot, Brian De Palma, David Fincher, Bong Joon-ho
Landmark films
The 39 Steps (1935), Rear Window (1954), Vertigo (1958), The Silence of the Lambs (1991)
Legacy
Established the grammar of suspense that horror, action and crime films still borrow

What to look for

  • Information withheld from characters, or from the audience
  • Ordinary people pulled into danger
  • Ticking clocks and tightening spaces
  • Point-of-view shots and long takes
  • Twists that recast what came before
