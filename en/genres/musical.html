Musical

In the musical, song and dance carry story and feeling. Hollywood's sound era made it a studio staple, from Busby Berkeley's geometric spectacles to the Astaire–Rogers films and the Technicolor masterpieces of Vincente Minnelli, Gene Kelly and Stanley Donen. The form flourished in India, where song is part of most popular films, and returned in the 2000s with Chicago and La La Land. Choreography, camera movement and editing are all designed to move with the music.

Where
The United States, India, France, Britain
When
From 1927; classic Hollywood peak 1930s–50s
Key figures
Busby Berkeley, Vincente Minnelli, Gene Kelly, Stanley Donen, Jacques Demy, Raj Kapoor
Landmark films
Top Hat (1935), Singin' in the Rain (1952), The Umbrellas of Cherbourg (1964), Mughal-e-Azam (1960)
Legacy
Made movement and music a way of telling story that other genres borrow

What to look for

  • Numbers that advance character or plot
  • Choreography built for the camera
  • Bold colour, costume and set design
  • Songs that express what dialogue cannot
  • Ensemble finales
