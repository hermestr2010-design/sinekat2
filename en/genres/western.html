Western

The Western is the great American myth on film: the frontier, the lone gunfighter, settlers and Native peoples, law arriving in a lawless land. John Ford gave it monumental landscapes; Anthony Mann and Budd Boetticher added psychological edge; Sergio Leone's Italian 'spaghetti' Westerns made it operatic and violent; Peckinpah and Eastwood later questioned the myth. Its themes of honour and violence echo in samurai, crime and science-fiction films.

Where
The United States; also Italy and Spain (spaghetti Westerns)
When
From 1903; golden age 1939–1960; spaghetti Westerns 1960s–70s
Key figures
John Ford, Howard Hawks, Anthony Mann, Sergio Leone, Sam Peckinpah, Clint Eastwood
Landmark films
The Great Train Robbery (1903), Stagecoach (1939), The Searchers (1956), Once Upon a Time in the West (1968)
Legacy
Gave cinema its most durable myth of individual justice and the cost of violence

What to look for

  • Landscape as character
  • The code of the lone hero
  • Showdowns staged like duels
  • Railroads, ranches and frontier towns
  • Revisionist doubts about the myth
