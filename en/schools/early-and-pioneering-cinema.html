1890s – 1920s

Early and Pioneering Cinema

The first generation of filmmakers invented the basic techniques of cinema: trick effects, editing, the feature film and the epic.

Cinema began as a fairground novelty and became an art in about thirty years. The Lumière brothers filmed everyday life in 1895, Georges Méliès turned the camera into a magician's tool, and Edwin S. Porter and D.W. Griffith worked out continuity editing, cross-cutting and the close-up that let a story be told through images alone. Alice Guy-Blaché and Lois Weber were among the first to direct fiction, and Louis Feuillade made serials that audiences followed week after week. By the 1920s the epic, the comedy and the feature film were all established, and Hollywood was becoming the industry's centre.

Where
France, the United States, with early work in Britain, Italy and Japan
When
1895 to the late 1920s
Key figures
Lumière brothers, Georges Méliès, Edwin S. Porter, D.W. Griffith, Alice Guy-Blaché, Lois Weber, Louis Feuillade
Landmark films
A Trip to the Moon (1902), The Great Train Robbery (1903), The Birth of a Nation (1915), Les Vampires (1915)
Legacy
Invented the grammar of editing, framing and storytelling that all later cinema still uses

What to look for

  • Trick effects, stage-like sets and painted backdrops
  • Single-reel shorts growing into feature-length epics
  • Acting and storytelling carried entirely by image and gesture
