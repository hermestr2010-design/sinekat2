Political and Investigative Cinema

Films that reconstruct real events and expose the workings of power, corruption and conspiracy, from the political thrillers of the 1960s and 1970s to modern docudrama.

Some films treat cinema as a tool of inquiry, reconstructing real events to ask who holds power and who pays for it. Gillo Pontecorvo's The Battle of Algiers (1966) filmed the Algerian war as if it were newsreel, Costa-Gavras made Z (1969) as a conspiracy thriller out of a real Greek assassination, and Francesco Rosi and Elio Petri examined Italian corruption in the 1960s and 70s. Later directors such as Oliver Stone, Paul Greengrass, Laura Poitras and Alexander Nanau continued the tradition, and Yılmaz Güney and Mohammad Rasoulof made political films under real personal risk.

What to look for

  • Reconstruction of real events, often in near-documentary style
  • Conspiracy, corruption and abuse of power as plots
  • A clear political viewpoint and a drive for public debate
