Polish Cinema

Polish film in and after the Communist era, from moral anxiety to humanist drama.

Polish film after 1945 was shaped by the war and by communist rule. In the 1950s the 'Polish School', led by Andrzej Wajda, Andrzej Munk and Wojciech Has, looked at the Resistance and occupation with irony and moral doubt, and Roman Polanski began with the strange, tense Knife in the Water. In the 1970s the 'Cinema of Moral Anxiety' of Krzysztof Zanussi, Wajda and Krzysztof Kieślowski examined everyday compromise in a regime of lies. After 1989, Kieślowski's Decalogue and Three Colours won world fame, and later directors such as Agnieszka Holland, Paweł Pawlikowski and Jerzy Skolimowski carried the tradition abroad.
