1960s

Spaghetti Western

Italian-made westerns with stylised violence, long silences and operatic scores.

In the mid-1960s Italian directors, shooting cheaply in Spain and Italy, took the American western and made it cynical, violent and stylised. Sergio Leone's A Fistful of Dollars launched the cycle in 1964 and his Dollars trilogy and Once Upon a Time in the West set its look: sweat-streaked close-ups, long silences, huge landscapes and Ennio Morricone's unmistakable music. Sergio Corbucci and Sergio Sollima added darker and more political versions. More than 400 films followed before the cycle faded in the late 1970s, but its influence runs through Peckinpah, Tarantino and modern action cinema.

Where
Italy and Spain, with international casts
When
1964 to the late 1970s
Key figures
Sergio Leone, Sergio Corbucci, Sergio Sollima, composer Ennio Morricone, actor Clint Eastwood
Landmark films
A Fistful of Dollars (1964), The Good, the Bad and the Ugly (1966), Once Upon a Time in the West (1968), Django (1966)
Legacy
Reshaped the western and gave modern cinema its taste for stylised, operatic violence

What to look for

  • Extreme close-ups of eyes and faces against vast landscapes
  • Anti-heroes, moral ambiguity and revenge
  • Bold, dominant music and long, tense silences
