1960s – 1980s

Soviet Poetic Cinema

Soviet directors who pursued spiritual, personal and poetic cinema despite official pressure.

After Stalin's death in 1953, Soviet cinema began to breathe again. In the 1960s and 70s directors from Georgia, Armenia and Ukraine made films full of metaphor, folk myth and painterly images, often working around censorship by speaking in symbols. Sergei Parajanov's The Colour of Pomegranates (1969) treated an Armenian poet's life as a series of living tableaux, Tarkovsky's Andrei Rublev and Mirror reflected on faith, memory and time, and Larisa Shepitko, Elem Klimov, Kira Muratova and Otar Iosseliani brought their own voices. Many of their films were cut, banned or shelved, and released only later.

Where
USSR, notably Russia, Ukraine, Georgia and Armenia
When
Late 1950s to the 1980s
Key figures
Andrei Tarkovsky, Sergei Parajanov, Larisa Shepitko, Elem Klimov, Kira Muratova, Otar Iosseliani, Tengiz Abuladze
Landmark films
Shadows of Forgotten Ancestors (1965), Andrei Rublev (1966), The Colour of Pomegranates (1969), Come and See (1985)
Legacy
Showed that film could be spiritual, painterly poetry, and strongly influenced later art cinema

What to look for

  • Image as poetry: slow movement, long takes and rich symbols
  • Religious, folk and historical themes
  • A deliberately indirect way of speaking about politics
