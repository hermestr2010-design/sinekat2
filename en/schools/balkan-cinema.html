Balkan Cinema

Films from the former Yugoslavia and its neighbours: the Black Wave and partisan epics, the comedies and tragedies of the wars of the 1990s, and the new generation of Balkan film-makers, with Romania, Greece, Bulgaria and Albania.

The Balkans produced very different cinemas that share a history of empire, war and rapid change. In Yugoslavia, the Black Wave of Dušan Makavejev, Aleksandar Petrović and Želimir Žilnik questioned the socialist state in the 1960s and 70s, while Emir Kusturica won Cannes twice with wild, bittersweet films. After the wars of the 1990s, directors in Bosnia, Serbia, Croatia, Kosovo and Macedonia, including Danis Tanović, Jasmila Žbanić and Milčo Mančevski, made films about memory and survival, and Romanian directors Cristian Mungiu, Cristi Puiu and Corneliu Porumboiu led a bleak, precise new wave. Greece, Bulgaria and Albania added their own voices.

Where
Yugoslavia and its successor states, Romania, Bulgaria, Albania and Greece
When
1950s to the present
Key figures
Emir Kusturica, Dušan Makavejev, Danis Tanović, Jasmila Žbanić, Cristian Mungiu, Cristi Puiu, Milčo Mančevski
Landmark films
WR: Mysteries of the Organism (1971), Underground (1995), No Man's Land (2001), 4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days (2007)
Legacy
Turned a history of conflict into a cinema of dark humour, realism and moral urgency

What to look for

  • Black humour and tragedy sitting side by side
  • War, memory and the pressure of the state
  • Direct, often rough realism, and in Romania very long takes
