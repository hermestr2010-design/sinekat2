Canadian Cinema

Filmmakers from Canada, from the Toronto New Wave and Winnipeg's silent-film dreamers to Quebec's young auteurs and directors who work in Hollywood.

Canadian film has long lived in the shadow of Hollywood, and it has often turned that distance into a style: cool, ironic and a little strange. The National Film Board, founded in 1939, built a world-leading tradition of documentary and animation. In the 1970s and 80s David Cronenberg created body horror in Toronto, Atom Egoyan made cool studies of grief and memory, and Guy Maddin imitated the shadows of silent cinema from Winnipeg. French-speaking Quebec gave Denys Arcand, Xavier Dolan and Denis Villeneuve, and Sarah Polley and Deepa Mehta show a continuing range of voices.
