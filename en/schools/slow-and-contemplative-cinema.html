Slow and Contemplative Cinema

Long takes and patience. Time itself becomes the subject.

Slow cinema asks viewers to give up the usual pace of film and watch time pass. Its films use very long takes, a still or slowly moving camera, little dialogue and sometimes almost no plot, and they treat boredom and waiting as part of experience. The tradition runs from Ozu, Antonioni and Tarkovsky to Chantal Akerman's Jeanne Dielman (1975), Béla Tarr's seven-hour Sátántangó (1994), and the work of Tsai Ming-liang, Apichatpong Weerasethakul, Lav Diaz, Pedro Costa and Nuri Bilge Ceylan. It is a cinema of patience that rewards close, quiet looking.

Where
Worldwide: Hungary, Taiwan, Thailand, the Philippines, Portugal, Turkey and more
When
1960s to the present, as a named trend from the 2000s

Béla Tarr, Chantal Akerman, Tsai Ming-liang, Apichatpong Weerasethakul, Lav Diaz, Pedro Costa
Landmark films
Jeanne Dielman (1975), Sátántangó (1994), Uncle Boonmee Who Can Recall His Past Lives (2010), The Turin Horse (2011)
Legacy
Made duration itself an artistic tool and gave festivals a distinct style of contemplative art film

What to look for

  • Very long takes with the camera barely moving
  • Minimal dialogue and little conventional plot
  • Everyday routines, silence and landscape
