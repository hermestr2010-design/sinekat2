1920s – today

Animation and Puppet Film

Directors who build whole worlds frame by frame, from stop-motion and puppet surrealism to hand-drawn and digital animation made by tiny teams.

Animation gives directors complete control over a world, built frame by frame, and it ranges far beyond children's films. Disney's Snow White (1937) showed that animation could carry a feature, and Japan's Studio Ghibli, led by Hayao Miyazaki and Isao Takahata, made richly human fantasies. Satoshi Kon, Katsuhiro Otomo and Makoto Shinkai added dream-like, science-fiction and romantic worlds, while Jan Švankmajer's stop-motion nightmares and Ari Folman's Waltz with Bashir showed animation as a tool for the unconscious and for memory. Gints Zilbalodis' Flow (2024) won the Oscar for Best Animated Feature.

What to look for

  • Fantasy, dream and memory as natural subjects
  • Total control of design, colour and movement
