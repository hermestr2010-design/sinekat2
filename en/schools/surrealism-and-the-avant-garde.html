Surrealism and the Avant-Garde

Dream logic, shocking imagery and a rejection of conventional storytelling.

Some filmmakers have always treated cinema as a form closer to painting, music or dreams than to theatre. In the 1920s Paris avant-garde, Man Ray, René Clair, Germaine Dulac and Jean Cocteau made short films about rhythm, chance and the unconscious, and Luis Buñuel and Salvador Dalí's Un chien andalou (1929) shocked audiences with dream images. The tradition continued with Buñuel's later satires, Jan Švankmajer's animated nightmares, Alejandro Jodorowsky's mystical films, Peter Greenaway and David Lynch. It is less a school than a repeated refusal of realism in favour of the irrational.

What to look for

  • Dream logic, odd images and no clear explanation
  • Attacks on bourgeois morality, religion and convention
  • Strong interest in the unconscious, desire and the body
