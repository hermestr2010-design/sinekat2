1980s – 1990s

Chinese Fifth Generation

Graduates of the Beijing Film Academy who made visually striking films about China's history, countryside and people.

In 1978 the Beijing Film Academy reopened after the Cultural Revolution, and its first graduating class, the 'Fifth Generation', went on to make a very different kind of Chinese film. Chen Kaige's Yellow Earth (1984), photographed by Zhang Yimou, used wide landscapes, long silences and bold colour to speak about tradition and rural poverty. Zhang Yimou's Red Sorghum won the Golden Bear in 1988, and Chen's Farewell My Concubine shared the 1993 Palme d'Or. Their stories of village life, family and political upheaval were often banned at home, but became the first Chinese films to win a world audience.

What to look for

  • Allegories of power that often met official censorship
