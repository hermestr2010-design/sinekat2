1930s – 1950s

French Poetic Realism and Classical Cinema

Humane, fluid and elegant French films that combined social observation with romance and fate.

In the 1930s French directors made films about working-class people, outsiders and doomed love, shot in studio streets and misty harbours with a melancholy beauty. Jean Renoir brought a humane, flowing camera to social subjects, Marcel Carné and the writer Jacques Prévert made fatalistic romances with Jean Gabin, and Jean Vigo made one wild, lyrical masterpiece, Zéro de conduite. In the years just after the war, directors such as Max Ophüls, Henri-Georges Clouzot and Robert Bresson continued the classical tradition, and the generation of the New Wave would later both attack and learn from it.
