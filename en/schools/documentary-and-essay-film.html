1920s – today

Documentary and Essay Film

Non-fiction directors who treat the camera as an instrument of observation and thought, from the city symphony and the film essay to long immersive portraits of places and people.

Documentary has always been more than recording the facts. Dziga Vertov's Man with a Movie Camera (1929) treated the camera as a new kind of eye, and Chris Marker's Sans Soleil (1983) pioneered the essay film, in which a narrator thinks aloud over images. Agnès Varda mixed autobiography and observation in The Gleaners and I (2000), Werner Herzog turned documentary into a search for ecstatic truth, and Michael Moore, Laura Poitras and Gianfranco Rosi extended its public reach. Jacques Cousteau's underwater films reached a mass audience, and today documentary is a major form of festival and streaming cinema.

Where
Worldwide, with strong traditions in the USSR, France, Britain, the United States and Germany
When
1920s to the present

Dziga Vertov, Chris Marker, Agnès Varda, Werner Herzog, Michael Moore, Laura Poitras, Gianfranco Rosi

Legacy
Showed that non-fiction can be personal, poetic and politically powerful

What to look for

  • Real people and places rather than actors and sets
  • Voice-over, commentary or the director's own presence
  • Mixing of observation, essay, memoir and argument
