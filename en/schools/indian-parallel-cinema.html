Indian Parallel Cinema

A realist, humanist alternative to mainstream Indian film, beginning with the Apu Trilogy.

While Bombay's studios made song-and-dance entertainment, a smaller group of Indian directors made realistic films about village life, poverty and social change. Satyajit Ray's Pather Panchali (1955), shot on a tiny budget with a mostly untrained cast, was the starting point. Ritwik Ghatak, Mrinal Sen, Mani Kaul, Shyam Benegal, Adoor Gopalakrishnan and Govind Nihalani worked in Bengali, Hindi and Malayalam, many with support from state film bodies in the 1960s and 1970s. The movement brought India's cinema to festivals and a world audience, and its realism still marks the independent films of today.

What to look for

  • Real locations, ordinary people and quiet storytelling
  • Poverty, caste, class and modernisation as themes
  • Little or no song and dance
