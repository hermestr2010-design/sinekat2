1960s

Czechoslovak New Wave

A short, brilliant flowering of Czech and Slovak cinema in the 1960s, with comedy, irony and non-professional actors, which ended with the Soviet invasion of 1968.

In the 1960s a group of young graduates of Prague's FAMU film school made some of the most playful and ironic films in Europe. Miloš Forman's Black Peter and The Fireman's Ball, Jiří Menzel's Closely Watched Trains, and Věra Chytilová's Daisies used absurd comedy, non-professional actors and sharp satire to poke at conformity and bureaucracy. Menzel's Closely Watched Trains won the Oscar for Best Foreign Language Film in 1968. The Soviet invasion of August 1968 ended it: films were banned, and Forman went to the United States, where he later made One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest.
