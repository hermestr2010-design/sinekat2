Commedia all'Italiana

Satirical Italian comedies that mix laughter with bitter social criticism of marriage, morals and politics.

At the height of Italy's postwar boom, a group of directors used comedy to look at the country's vanity, hypocrisy and greed. Mario Monicelli's The Big Deal on Madonna Street, Dino Risi's Il sorpasso and Pietro Germi's Divorce Italian Style were funny, but the laughter stung: the heroes are cowards, cheats and failures, and the endings are often bitter. The films relied on great comic actors such as Alberto Sordi, Vittorio Gassman and Marcello Mastroianni and on sharp writing, and they gave Italians a mirror of the national character in a time of rapid change.

What to look for

  • Satire of the national character, with flawed, selfish heroes
  • Comedy mixed with a sad or bitter ending
  • Brilliant ensemble acting and sharp scripts
