Iranian New Wave

Poetic realism and moral complexity from Iran, often blurring fiction and documentary.

In the late 1960s Dariush Mehrjui's The Cow started a new Iranian cinema of poetic realism and social concern, and after the 1979 revolution a generation of filmmakers learned to work within strict rules. Abbas Kiarostami blurred fiction and documentary in Close-Up and Taste of Cherry, Mohsen Makhmalbaf and Majid Majidi found drama among the poor and children, and Jafar Panahi and Mohammad Rasoulof made films about life under pressure, in some cases despite bans and arrest. Asghar Farhadi's A Separation won the Oscar for Best Foreign Language Film in 2012. The cinema uses indirect storytelling to say what cannot be said openly.

What to look for

  • Children, ordinary people and moral choices in simple settings
  • Blending of fiction and documentary, with real people playing themselves
  • Indirect storytelling that works around censorship
