Brazil

Brazilian cinema found its voice with Cinema Novo in the 1960s, when Glauber Rocha called for a cinema of hunger, a low-budget, politically angry cinema about poverty and inequality. After the dictatorship, directors such as Walter Salles and Fernando Meirelles mixed social realism with polished storytelling, and City of God showed the country's cinema to the world.
