Russia / USSR

Soviet cinema was shaped by revolution. In the 1920s Eisenstein, Pudovkin, Vertov and Kuleshov developed montage theory and treated editing as the main source of meaning. Stalin's demand for socialist realism narrowed the field, yet directors such as Dovzhenko and later Kalatozov found poetic ways within it. After the Thaw of the 1950s a more personal cinema returned, and national film schools in places like Georgia and Armenia flourished. Post-Soviet Russian cinema had to reinvent itself after the collapse of state funding. Sokurov, Mikhalkov and Zvyagintsev have made some of its best-known work, with a taste for long, painterly images, moral allegory and religious and political themes, drawing on a Tarkovsky-like inheritance. The industry now mixes popular blockbusters with a small art cinema, under changing political pressures.
